01 / ONE SHARED SPACE

Start with “us”.
Know what comes next.

Cup brings the practical and personal parts of life together into one place. The home overview is the starting point: see what needs attention, then move into a plan, a shared expense or a conversation.

DESIGN QUESTION

How might everyday coordination feel like caring for each other?

The rose palette and conversational tone make the space feel personal. Clear dates, owners and status labels keep the next action easy to find.

03 / DESIGN FOR TWO EQUAL VOICES

Shared does not
mean assumed.

Plans and spending make responsibilities visible. Feedback creates room to discuss them: a suggestion stays a proposal until both people confirm. Try one confirmation, then the other, and undo either to see the difference.

TRY A MUTUAL AGREEMENT

Let’s choose our next day out
on Friday evening.

An idea for now. Both people need to confirm.

Select a confirmed partner again to withdraw confirmation. The proposal then returns to a waiting state.
01Location, by choice.

Send a check-in or enable time-limited sharing. Clear stop controls keep the choice with the person sharing.

02Reflection, with permission.

The optional AI journal uses selected records and a configured provider. People choose the sources to include.

03A proposal is not an agreement.

A response from one partner is visible, but it does not count as approval from both. The status must distinguish waiting from mutually confirmed.

04 / CLOSE THE LOOP

Everyday actions.
A shared history.

The journey does not end when a task is checked off. Search brings plans, spending, wishes, feedback and weekly journals back into view. Reflection can become the starting point for the next conversation or plan.

REVISIT

Find the moment.

Search across the shared records to return to a plan, an expense or a conversation. Keep practical information and personal moments connected.

REFLECT

Choose what to include.

An optional AI journal helps reflect on the week using records selected by the user. Reflection is a choice, with control over its sources.

NEXT DESIGN VALIDATION

Test the whole journey.

Observe whether couples can turn a wish into a task, understand a custom split and distinguish a proposal from a confirmed agreement. These are proposed checks, not measured outcomes.

Designed by Cici Song and Peter. Cup means couple. This portfolio presents the product journey using English interface templates and fictional examples adapted from the web design preview. The interactive walkthrough illustrates the documented features; it is not a connected account. The web product is available and the native iOS client is in development.
