01 / RESOLVE THE SEAT AS A SYSTEM

A complete seat.
Individually considered parts.

Vela is a modular economy-seat concept that brings structure, comfort and service access together. Switch views to see the complete seat, then follow its disassembly tree below.

PASSENGER EXPERIENCE

Comfort is a
design requirement.

Contoured cushions, lumbar support and head pillows target posture and pressure relief within economy-class space. Their comfort performance still needs passenger testing.

MODULAR ARCHITECTURE

A structure designed
to be accessed.

The aluminium frame and separable modules connect the low-mass ambition with service access. Replacing a component is considered alongside assembling the complete seat.

SERVICE & REPLACEMENT

Design the back
for the next repair.

Removable covers, fasteners and separable modules avoid bonded joints. The intention is easier repair, cleaning and material recovery throughout the seat’s life.

03 / FROM SUSTAINABILITY TO REQUIREMENTS

The seat’s story
continues after landing.

The project starts with a repair and recovery problem: bonded, multi-material seats are difficult to separate. Vela explores how product architecture can address that challenge while keeping passenger comfort central.

THE DESIGN QUESTION

How might an economy seat be lightweight, modular, economic and circular without compromising comfort?

MASS TARGET / PER PASSENGER≤ 10 kg

A design requirement that guides the structure and material choices.

EXPLORE THE DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

01 Replaceable, separable modules

Consider repair from the beginning. Cushions and panels should be accessible as individual components.

02 Recycled, recoverable materials

Material selection and separation should support recovery at the end of the seat’s service life.

03 Ergonomic short-haul support

Develop posture and pressure-relief features within the available economy-class space.

DESIGN PRINCIPLEConsider the passenger and the product’s next life together.

04 / DEVELOP THE MODULAR STRUCTURE

Less mass.
More thought in every joint.

A hollow aluminium frame and X-support form the structural direction. Explore the original CAD studies, then see how replaceable comfort modules connect to the frame.

STRUCTURAL DIRECTION

Start with a lighter frame.

Hollow aluminium establishes the lightweight direction. The aim is to reduce mass while keeping a structure that can support replaceable seat modules.

STRUCTURAL DEVELOPMENT

Support the load.
Keep the structure open.

The X-support develops the load-bearing base around the low-mass objective. Structural strength remains a requirement to verify through load testing.

INTEGRATED STRUCTURE

Make the base
part of the system.

The developed base brings supports and connecting members together. It provides the foundation for a seat that can be assembled, accessed and separated.

FROM STRUCTURE TO REPLACEABLE PARTS

Comfort modules that can come apart.

Clip-on cushions and removable panels are intended to simplify replacement, repair and material separation.

05 / REFLECT & DEFINE THE NEXT VALIDATION

A resolved concept.
A clear path to validation.

Vela connects a sustainability goal with product requirements, a modular CAD assembly and a recovery strategy. The next step is to test how those design intentions perform in use.

VALIDATION PENDING / EXPLORE NEXT STEPS

01 Structure & materials

Load and flammability tests remain pending. The CAD assembly and material analysis define a concept to take into this validation.

02 Passenger comfort

Comfort testing is needed to assess the contoured cushions, lumbar support and head pillows in the intended short-haul setting.

03 Maintenance & recovery

Maintenance and recovery trials need to assess how the proposed modules, fasteners and separation routes work in practice.
