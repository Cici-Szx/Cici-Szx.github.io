01 / BRING THE SYSTEM TOGETHER

A path to follow.
A place to make your own.

Jìng reimagines Queen’s Lawn as a place to rest, study and connect. Explore the final CAD proposal by day, by night and within the site, then see how campus research and co-design shaped its curved landscape and modular seating.

HOW THE DESIGN RESPONDS

01 Shelter & dry ground

Flooring and drainage were added following feedback about wet grass and water collecting along the structure. Sheltered and open areas offer a choice of conditions.

02 Study & social use

Modular seating and tables support different group sizes. Curved boundaries aim to create smaller gathering spaces while retaining connections between them.

03 Play & campus identity

A slide and climbing wall respond to requests for playful activity. Display surfaces connect the installation with campus culture.

PROPOSED MATERIALS

Made for outdoor use.

Recycled plastic composite for the modular seats; polycarbonate for transparent components. These selections refine the earlier teak and HDPE exploration.

02 / UNDERSTAND EVERYDAY CAMPUS LIFE

A busy route.
An inconsistent place.

The lawn connects teaching spaces and the library, yet staying there depends on the weather. Research found limited seating, muddy ground and few places that comfortably support both individual and group use.

45Student survey responses
14Interview participants
15Initial ideas explored

Discovery report, pp. 3, 9–10. Interviews included 10 students, 3 maintenance staff and 1 professor.

FROM OBSERVATIONS TO DESIGN NEEDS

01 Make staying comfortable

Students improvised seats and eating surfaces when space was scarce. More usable seating and tables became a core requirement.

02 Offer a choice of conditions

Mud, rain and seasonal shade limited use. The design needed dry surfaces, shelter and options for both sun and shade.

03 Support different social needs

Quiet rest, studying and group conversation compete for the same space. Layout and movable seating should support different ways of gathering.

03 / TURN INSIGHTS INTO A DIRECTION

Explore broadly.
Choose with purpose.

Fifteen initial ideas led to three developed concepts. The glass house ranked strongly in early ideation; the later review favoured The Path, with a better feasibility rating and room for a shared spatial experience.

ALTERNATIVE CONCEPT

A compact place to pause.

A rotating multifunctional bench explored seating, an adjustable backrest, a foldable table and charging. Its smaller intervention offers flexible use, but the brief also calls for a shared spatial experience.

04 / BUILD, INVOLVE, REVISE

Test the space.
Then test the seat.

The team worked at two scales: foam and cardboard models to explore spatial relationships, and a full-size cardboard seat to understand comfort, shared use and modularity.

05 / WHAT THE PROCESS CHANGED

Design a place.
Let people shape it.

The process moved from adding facilities to understanding how people want to occupy a shared space. Co-design changed both the overall structure and the way the seat works.

LEARNING

Lower can connect more.

Workshop feedback challenged the elevated loop. The path was preferred for its relationship between privacy and social connection.

LEARNING

Flexibility can be simpler.

Testing shifted attention from a folding mechanism to separable modules and how people might arrange them together.

NEXT VALIDATION

Test beyond the model.

Further work should evaluate full-scale structure, access, outdoor durability, drainage and everyday use before considering a built installation.

EVIDENCE & LIMITS

The reports document research, physical prototypes, participant feedback and a final CAD proposal. They do not establish long-term use, verified weather performance or the capacity of a built installation.
