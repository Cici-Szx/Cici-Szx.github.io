01 / DEVELOP THE AERODYNAMIC PACKAGE

Every feature
has a purpose.

Aura combines a streamlined SUV body, a Kammback rear and front air curtains. Switch views to see each feature and its intended aerodynamic role; the CFD evidence later in the story shows what still needs refinement.

DESIGN INTENT / OVERALL FORM

Start with
surface continuity.

The raked front, smooth roof-to-rear transition and clean side surfacing are intended to maintain attached flow and limit local separation.

DESIGN INTENT / REAR SEPARATION

Give the airflow
a defined exit.

The Kammback combines a tapered rear with a sharp cut-off and rear concavity. The aim is to anchor separation; the subsequent CFD still showed a thick wake.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE CONSTRAINTS

A larger body.
A more complex flow.

The study starts with the SUV’s height and frontal area. Aerodynamic drag and crosswind response become core design constraints, shaping both the overall form and its local features.

THE DESIGN QUESTION

How might we use body shape and airflow control to reduce SUV drag without compromising stability?

THREE LOSS ZONES / EXPLORE THE PRIORITIES

01 Rear wake

The project identifies low base pressure behind the vehicle as a source of pressure drag. Rear separation becomes a central design priority.

02 Wheels & underbody

Turbulence around the wheels and lower body adds local losses. These areas inform the air-curtain and lower-body design direction.

03 Surface continuity

Breaks in the body surface can trigger flow separation. Aura’s base form focuses on smoother transitions.

DESIGN PRIORITYConsider drag and stability together.

05 / RESOLVE, THEN REFINE

One airflow strategy.
An open design loop.

The final concept brings a streamlined body, Kammback rear and air curtains together. Its value lies in connecting a clear design intention with evidence that informs the next iteration.

Design for a system

Body form and local airflow features were developed as one package, rather than isolated styling gestures.

Use evidence honestly

The CFD redirects attention to a remaining weakness: the broad rear wake. It becomes a design input for the next loop.

Keep the next step clear

The source identifies rear wake, underbody and wheel flow as the priorities for further refinement.

NEXT LOOP / FROM THE STUDY

Refine the wake, lower-body flow and wheel region. Assess drag and stability together as the concept develops.
